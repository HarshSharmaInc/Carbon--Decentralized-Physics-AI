01 · The Pitch

A subnet that sells fast physics, not raw tokens

Miners run physics-informed AI surrogates (neural operators trained to approximate PDE solutions) in place of classical FEM/CFD solvers. An engineer submits a geometry and a loading condition and gets a stress or displacement field back in milliseconds instead of a multi-hour solve.

Constraint 01

Verification is the bottleneck

A full ground-truth solve is exactly the cost we're trying to remove: we can't run one on every request without defeating the point.

Constraint 02

Registration is a sunk cost

Current dynamic registration burn runs $1.5–2.5M+, non-refundable, and roughly doubles with each new subnet.

Constraint 03

Hardware access isn't uniform

Consumer GPU, cloud GPU, and TPU miners are different populations with different reach; the design has to pick who it's open to.

02 · Prior Art

Carbon isn't the first physics subnet

Subnet 25 (Mainframe) solved the same trust problem for protein folding, and just partnered with Rowan Scientific to generate DFT data for neural network potentials. Same pitch, one science over.

04 · Why This Can Work

Expensive to solve, cheap to verify

The same asymmetry that lets SN25 score a protein by its energy lets Carbon score a solution by its PDE residual: checking correctness is far cheaper than producing a correct answer.

Tier 1 · every response

Physics-gate residuals computed directly on the miner's output field: mass & momentum conservation, boundary-condition satisfaction, rollout stability. Milliseconds, runs every tempo.

Tier 2 · sampled

Comparison against a held-out classical FEM/CFD solve, drawn from a rotating problem bank the validator doesn't reveal in advance. Catches degenerate solutions that satisfy Tier 1 without actually solving the problem.

Tier 1 alone is gameable: a near-constant field can satisfy conservation trivially. Tier 2 is the backstop that makes gate-passing mean something.
05 · Miner Side

Neural operators, not language models

The model zoo already exists; it doesn't need to be invented for Carbon, only adapted to run against sampled challenges.

FNOTFNOGNO GINOWNOHNO TransolverGAOT

All eight are already implemented in JAX in production use at ZenteiQ: that's a working starting model zoo, not a research bet.

Inference for these operators runs on a single commodity GPU. That's a real barrier-to-entry advantage over LLM subnets, which typically assume multi-GPU or H100 clusters just to serve.
06 · Framework & Hardware

One codebase, two backends, used differently on each side

Miner side: stay hardware-agnostic

JAX/XLA compiles the same model to GPU or TPU with no rewrite. Don't require TPU for mining: TPU access is cloud/TRC-gated, not consumer hardware, so mandating it narrows the miner pool against Bittensor's own decentralization incentive.

Validator side: TPU is the clearer win

Tier-2 ground-truth generation is the real throughput bottleneck: the validator has to mass-produce and verify held-out solves fast enough to keep up with miner traffic. pmap/pjit batching across a TPU pod scales that well, and FNO-style spectral convolutions (FFT + dense matmul) map cleanly onto the MXU.

Recommendation: JAX everywhere as the framework choice. TPU where the validator needs batch throughput. GPU-first for miners, to keep the network open.
07 · Incentives

Gate first, then score, then shape the curve

Three separate decisions, easy to conflate into one, so it's worth pulling apart before locking a mechanism.

eligible = tier1_gate(residual) == PASS score = w1 · tier2_physics_accuracy + w2 · latency_efficiency # only if eligible weight = reward_curve(score) # Top-K / 80-20, or continuous (open)

SN25 uses Top-K, winner-take-most: sharper competition, higher variance, discourages small miners. Continuous scoring is gentler but slower to punish mediocre solutions. Bittensor also supports multiple mechanisms per subnet: separate bond pools for fast-inference vs. high-fidelity miners instead of one curve forcing a single speed/accuracy tradeoff on everyone.
